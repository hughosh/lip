## Current program and market evidence

The [July 15 amendment](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/regulatory/notices/Liquidity%20Incentive%20Program%20-%20July%2015%2C%202026%20Update.pdf)
states an effective date of **July 30, 2026**. The
[July 30 clean terms](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/regulatory/notices/Liquidity%20Incentive%20Program%20-%20July%2030%2C%202026%20Modification%20to%20July%2015%2C%202026%20Update.pdf)
extend the published end to **January 1, 2027**, subject to amendment or early
termination. These were located on the live [regulatory notices page](https://kalshi.com/regulatory/notices),
downloaded and read directly; the clean terms' date was also visually checked.
The [help page](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)
agrees on the date and scoring, but still links the obsolete February PDF.

Under the July terms, the reference price is the first descending bid level
where cumulative depth reaches one fifth of Target Size. Qualifying levels
extend through the level reaching the full target. Scores use size times
`discount_factor ^ max(distance_below_reference_in_ticks, 0)`, normalized per
side. Snapshots occur once per second at a nonpublic random offset renewed
periodically. Closed markets or insufficient depth on either side exclude the
snapshot. The period payout is score share × reward × eligible-snapshot
fraction, paid only at $1 or more, floored to cents. These conditions differ
materially from the historical economics note.

Account eligibility is a separate pre-pilot check: the July clean appendix
excludes IB/FCM participation, while the help page additionally describes U.S.
eligibility and affiliate/employee exclusions. No account-specific eligibility
has been established here.

empty terminal cursor. All periods contained that observation time. At
17:47:43–17:47:44 UTC, the new Go inspector completed **eight pages, 7,026

## Repairs and useful prerequisites

`rest.Client.Programs` now requests `status=active&type=liquidity` on every
cursor page. Previously, an active volume program without `target_size_fp`
could invalidate the entire walk and prevent a valid liquidity ticker from
starting. This is a **latent compatibility defect**, not a current outage:
there were no active volume programs in today's read. The conformance probe
now requests the same production filters; its wider probe remains explicitly
latent. Malformed liquidity targets still refuse the entire walk.

`go/cmd/incentives` is a new public-only inspection command. It reads no key,
environment credential file, harness config, or database. It permits only GETs
to incentives, market, event, and series paths on the fixed public API host;
redirects, writes, private routes, incomplete walks, malformed programs, and
HTTP errors fail closed. Output preserves raw reward/scoring/fee fields and
records start/end UTC times. `complete` means pagination completed, not that a
market or account is approved for trading. Example:

```sh
cd /Users/hugh/kek/lip/go
GOPROXY=off GOSUMDB=off go build -o /private/tmp/lip-incentives ./cmd/incentives
/private/tmp/lip-incentives -ticker KXTTELITEMATCH-26SEP261405AWOMOL-AWO
```

The historical economics note now carries a dated warning and links here;
its original measurements are preserved. `config.example.md` now reflects
the implemented inventory/P&L stop consumers and startup fundability check,
while keeping the rung-based first-fill bound and qualification requirements.

Mutation verification also exposed a load-sensitive test fixture: the ping
store's durability wait exhausted 200,000 scheduler yields in 0.42 seconds

## Strategy assessment

**Verified facts:** rewards are active; the current rules dilute rewards by
competition and excluded time; fees differ by series. Historical repository
measurements are observational: 40,392 public fills over 6.5 hours on July 25,
dominated by one category. Their trade-through subset reports touch-price
markouts of −1.771¢ at 1 minute, −0.733¢ at 5 minutes, and −0.320¢ at 30 minutes.
The positive print-price report used a different, inappropriate entry price.
These numbers were extracted from the preserved note, not independently
recomputed from the collector databases, and are not our private fill results.

Net expected value must be evaluated as:

```text
E[attributable incentive payout]
  + E[realized exit proceeds - entry cost + value/change of residual inventory]
  - E[entry maker/taker fees + exit maker/taker fees + operating/capital costs].
```

Spread, depth slippage, and adverse selection determine executable entry/exit
prices in this expression; subtracting them again after using those prices
would double-count them. Unsold inventory needs a stated liquidation or
settlement valuation and a stress loss, not a convenient midprice profit.
Reward attribution needs actual order visibility over qualifying snapshots,
competitive normalized share on both sides, downtime/cancel latency, minimum
payout effects, and realized account credits. A headline market pool is not
our revenue.

**Estimates, not an edge:** on the saved book, a hypothetical 12-contract quote
on each side at the references has about 16.49% normalized share per side.
Freezing all other orders and maintaining it for the entire $20 period gives
about **$3.29** after flooring. One tick below each reference gives about
8.98% per side and **$1.79**. These are arithmetic counterfactuals assuming
100% qualifying time, constant competitors, no fills, and full-period uptime;
they are not achievable-payout forecasts. At 60% qualifying time and 25%
participation, the first calculation is below $1 and yields no payment. At an
illustrative net loss of 2.5¢ per completed contract, $3.29 only offsets about
132 contracts of turnover before other costs. The input hash, recomputed
reference levels, weighted denominators, and both counterfactuals are saved in
[`quote-counterfactual.json`](revival-evidence-2026-09-26/quote-counterfactual.json).

The historical 1-second versus 60-second requote calculation retained 99.62%
versus 94.71% of ideal score under its old assumptions. It suggests a slower,
threshold-based quote baseline is worth testing, but does not establish its
current reward share or fill cost. The exit-comparison scripts contain no
saved result sufficient to choose rapid exit over holding; one would value
exit at a future mid, not an executable bid. No authenticated analysis was run.

**Hypotheses:** book momentum might identify adverse fills early enough to
cancel, but feed delay, cancel acknowledgements/races, replenishment and queue
position can erase the benefit. Cancellation also loses both snapshot uptime
and queue priority; avoiding all fills can leave only the most adverse fills.
Public trades do not establish private fills or queue rank. Rapid taker exits
pay fees and may walk thin depth; maker exits save that fee only if they fill,
and retain inventory/settlement risk while waiting. The sampled 57¢ spread
shows a very wide market, but is not itself the loss on a maker-entry/taker-exit
round trip. That loss depends on the executable depth remaining after our
fill; quick flattening cannot be assumed cheap.

A credible comparison uses timestamped exchange/local book events, sequence
gaps, decision/send/ack/cancel times, owned private fills, fee debits, and
reward credits. Compare fixed/deeper/threshold quotes with identical limits;
compare immediate executable taker exits with capped maker exits and a bounded
holding timeout. Split chronologically by market/day, freeze momentum features
and parameters before the test window, purge overlapping outcome horizons,
account for real latency and uncertain queue allocation, and report net EV,
tail inventory losses, and confidence intervals. The momentum version must
beat these simpler baselines out of sample after all costs. **Profitability is
unproven; further measurement is justified, unattended capital deployment is
not justified by the available evidence.**
