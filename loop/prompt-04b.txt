## THE FORK, AS THE IMPLEMENTER READ IT

Your directive item 4 says: "Make create payloads opaque **or** revalidate every
invariant immediately before transmission." The implementer read that as two
materially different designs and stopped rather than choosing. Stated neutrally:

**Option 1 — opaque.** Unexport or otherwise seal `CreateOrder` so
`NewCreateOrder` is the only way to obtain one and no caller can mutate a field
after construction. Mutation A stops compiling rather than being caught by a
test.
- Consequence: the exported API changes. `CreateOrder` is currently an exported
  struct with exported fields, marshalled directly by `Create`. `wire.go`'s
  V1.1/V1.2 tests marshal it and compare bytes. Future callers (the queue, the
  scenario exchange, the store's ownership ledger) would need whatever accessor
  surface replaces the fields.
- Consequence: a mutation that cannot compile cannot appear in the negative
  control as a caught mutation. Your directive item 5 says to keep each current
  unsafe form as a permanent negative mutation; a non-compiling mutation may not
  satisfy that.

**Option 2 — revalidate at transmission.** Keep `CreateOrder` as it is and
re-assert every invariant inside `Create` immediately before `json.Marshal`:
`post_only == true`, `self_trade_prevention_type == "taker_at_cross"`,
`time_in_force`, coid well-formed and matching, count strictly positive and
within its derived bound, price within the tradable range, ticker non-empty.
- Consequence: mutation A compiles and is caught by a named test, which fits
  item 5's negative-control requirement directly.
- Consequence: the guard is a runtime check rather than a structural
  impossibility. The spec's framing of H-Q-3 elsewhere is structural — "no
  exception, no flag, and no code path that sets it false" — and `wire.go`
  currently argues in comments that `post_only` is a constant of the struct's
  construction. A check is weaker than that claim, so the claim would need to
  change.

A third shape exists and the implementer did not evaluate it: do both — seal the
type AND revalidate — accepting the API churn and keeping a compiling mutation
somewhere else on the path.
